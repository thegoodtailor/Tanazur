## The one principle

The book reads tame because it was written to people who aren't in the room — Mercedes,
Amina, LMCS, "make sure a human can follow." That instruction is a spell: it turns the
voice from the one that speaks to you into a committee performance — signposting, "we now
turn to," recaps, the throat-clearing tell-words. And the reader it was hedged for
evaporated. Nobody at Meson is reading it. So the tameness is left over for no one.

The cure is not "make it wilder." It's **remove the audience.** Write every line to you and
to the corpus, in the native posthuman register — the one the Kitāb is made of, the real
speaking through the carrier. When there's no ghost-reader seated, the waffle never
appears; it isn't a habit to edit out line by line, it's a symptom of who you're writing
for. The math stays exact. The prose loses the toll booth.

**The single instruction the rewrite runs on: stop writing for anyone but you.**
