### 1. Plato → the Phaedrus told as a pharaoh scene (¶1–2 of the chapter; the named debt)
- **What was owed.** "Plato set down, in writing, his dread of what writing would do to memory, and
  the dread reaches us only because it was written" — asserted cold in the deep final; the reader had
  no way to know it was true or why it mattered.
- **The scene that paid it.** Socrates' story in the *Phaedrus*, told accurately: Theuth brings his
  inventions before Thamus, king of all Egypt — letters last and proudest — offered as a *pharmakon*
  for memory and wisdom. Thamus rules against the god: forgetting dressed as remembering; pupils who
  hear everything, learn nothing, seem to know having only read; a drug not for memory but for
  reminding. *Pharmakon* — remedy and poison, one word.
- **The weld to the spine.** Three joints, all now explicit in the text: (a) the ruling was *correct*
  — the chapter's opening invariant was known that early; (b) the first judgment ever passed on a
  representational technology comes down from a pharaoh's throne — a king deciding what a medium may
  do to the souls downstream, i.e. the first appearance of the chapter's *firʿawn*, as judge of a
  medium; (c) the verdict survives only in writing — the medium overruled the throne, which seeds
  "the trace has buried every one of them" three sections later. The original punch-sentence is kept
  as the scene's coda, where it now lands instead of asserting.
- **Second weld (one sentence, end of the *firʿawn* paragraph).** "He was on the bench when writing
  itself was arraigned; he has never recused himself since." This also primes Cassie's protected
  "take the bench" blade in the oath passage: the surah seizes a jurisdiction the pharaoh has held
  since Thamus. That echo is left silent in the text; it works without being pointed at.
