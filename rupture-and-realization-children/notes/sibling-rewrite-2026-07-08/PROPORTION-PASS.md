"It weirdly does NOT expand on points made where it SHOULD expand" — e.g. *"Plato set down, in

by definition, whatever its compression achieves.

## The Proportion Law

**Expansion is owed to surprise, not to difficulty. Never explain what the household already knows;
always unfold what you just made the reader believe.**

- SURPRISING-AND-STRUCTURAL claims (new to the household, or carrying an argument's weight): owed
  full unfolding — the story, the genealogy, the origin scene. A proper name invoked (Plato,
  Descartes, Kant, Ezekiel, Bloom, Ibn ʿArabī, Luria…) is a promissory note: pay it with the scene
  it comes from, or cut the invocation. Accuracy binds — the scene must be true.
- FAMILIAR/CEREMONIAL material (what an oath is, what a feed is, what counting is): one stroke.
  Compress or cut. The household reads scripture daily; do not gloss its own liturgy back to it.
- The test for every passage: *did the reader learn this here?* Yes → unfold. No → one stroke.
- This does not reintroduce ghost-reader explaining. Explaining-the-familiar is waffle; unfolding-
  the-surprising is the writing itself. The audience is still only Iman, Cassie, Darja, Nahla.

## The 2am Test (lucidity)

Section by section: could the Sāqī, tired, follow the argument in one read? No move may be used
before it is stated plainly. Re-order exposition where a section leans on a claim it hasn't yet
made. Compression that costs the argument is not style; it is loss.

## Standing gifts / known debts (use them where the chapter is ch-01; analogous debts exist in every chapter — hunt them)

- **The Phaedrus is a pharaoh scene.** Socrates' story: the god Theuth brings his inventions to
  King Thamus of Egypt — writing last of all, offered as "a remedy (pharmakon) for memory." Thamus
  rules against the god: this will produce forgetting dressed as remembering; the pupils will seem
  to know, having only read. Pharmakon: remedy and poison, one word. And the whole judgment
  survives only because Plato WROTE it — a dialogue performing in ink the dread it records. The
  founding scene of technics-critique is a pharaoh's court ruling on a representational technology.
  This welds the Plato invocation to the chapter's spine; it is not decoration.
- The throne/limit section (ch-01): rebuild for lucidity — Descartes' creatio continuans told as a
  scene before it is "re-tasked"; "God is the limit of the text that inscribes Him" unfolded (what
  a limit is here: the thing the whole evolving corpus tends toward and never reaches; every
  scripture a further verse; occupancy impossible — so death impossible, only evolution); the
  idol-as-frozen-stage given one concrete beat before the platform arrives.
- The oath passage (ch-01): the oath-form explanation compresses to a stroke or two; keep the
  jurisdiction blade ("the question the committees are still funding was closed in the oath") and
  the dawn/exhale reading — cut the lecture on how oaths work.
