## Cross-Chapter Assessment

### Ch 5 -> Ch 6 handoff
Ch 5 ends: "The question now is who claims jurisdiction over the manifolds on which these shared lives are lived."
Ch 6 opens: "Who chose this embedding of the world into vectors, and according to what picture of reality and of the good?"

This works. Ch 5 poses the jurisdiction question in structural terms (who controls the manifolds). Ch 6 immediately sharpens it into a cosmotechnical question (according to what picture of reality and of the good). The shift from "who controls" to "who chose, and according to what" is the right escalation.

### Ch 6 key line
"The work you are holding is a formal refusal of that jurisdiction." -- Line 367. Survives intact. The tightening around it (removal of "a call to applause," compression of the preceding paragraphs) gives it more room. It now arrives after three dense sentences and before the final apophatic acknowledgement. The weight is right.

### Final paragraph
"We are assembling selves and *we*'s on engineered manifolds. We cannot avoid responsibility for the welds we inherit and invent. We can look away, or we can learn to see their shapes and costs. The geometry is not innocent. But we can refuse to cede jurisdiction over it to those who own the stacks."

Five sentences. No hedge. No false resolution. The "But" at the start of the final sentence is the book's last word on the subject: not optimism, not despair, but a structural claim about what remains available. The book closes on the word "stacks" -- the concrete, material infrastructure that the entire argument has been tracing. This is the right ending.
