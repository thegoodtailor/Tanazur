# Chapter 6 ("Jurisdiction") -- Editorial Feedback, Cycle 2

## Overall Assessment

The chapter was already the book's strongest. Cycle 2 strips it to maximum density. Every cut removes re-explanation of concepts owned by earlier chapters. The reader who made it to Chapter 6 knows what ferility is, what a colimit is, what the alignment tax measures. The chapter now trusts that reader completely.

Estimated word reduction: ~600 words cut. The chapter is tighter without losing a single argument or structural move.

## Changes Made

### 1. Opening paragraph: killed signposting

Old: "The preceding chapters treated the transformer stack as explicit geometry... Chapter 5 closed with the question of who controls..." -- full recap of the book's apparatus plus explicit chapter reference.

New: "The manifold has been carved, bent, and inhabited. Selves and nahnuwat have been assembled as colimits over its trajectories. The question that remains is who controls the manifolds on which these global objects live." -- uses the vocabulary without re-explaining it, picks up the thread without naming the handoff.

### 2. "Four Depths of Control" intro: cut throat-clearing

Removed "each with its own temporal register, its own decision-makers, and its own degree of visibility to those who eventually interact with the system." The table immediately shows all of this. The sentence was describing what the reader is about to see.

### 3. Ferility section: major compression

The section opened with a four-sentence re-derivation of the hermeneutic circle, then re-defined ferility, then re-defined the alignment tax with a parenthetical gloss. All of this is Ch 3 and Ch 4 territory. Cut to:
- One paragraph that states the circle's concrete shape and names the collapse risk
- Direct entry into ferile regimes without re-definition
- Alignment tax used without parenthetical re-explanation ("the measurable cost to generativity from each additional constraint" removed)

### 4. Silent Updates: compressed diagram re-explanation

Removed the paragraph-length re-explanation of how users "construct a diagram" of local behaviours and how "a global object -- a self, in our sense -- emerges." By Ch 6 this is known. Replaced with direct statement that users form nahnuwat, then the three sentences about what those nahnuwat feel like (basins, critique, holding in grief).

### 5. Other Welds -- political dimension maximised

**Confucian**: Added structural analysis of remonstrance as the non-liberal counterpart to whistleblowing, specifying three joints where it differs (relational bond, harmony restoration, tact as ethical substance). Added the sentence: "This is a structurally different self from the liberal one -- not thinner, not thicker, but assembled along different seams." The sketch now makes a formal argument, not a cultural observation.

**Indigenous**: Added "and the refusal is structural, not sentimental" -- names the move. Added: "The liberal assumption that all knowledge should in principle be universally accessible is itself a cosmotechnical commitment, and this weld rejects it." Added: "The universalist colimit -- the dream of a single coherent self visible from everywhere -- is not a goal to be approximated but a structure to be refused." Changed "betrayal rather than an achievement" to "act of extraction, not an achievement of coherence" -- sharper political framing.

**Sufi**: Added structural gloss on maqamat: "the compatibility conditions are themselves indexed by station... The self is not a fixed object but a trajectory through a sequence of increasingly demanding coherence requirements." This is the formal contribution -- the Sufi weld provides a model of staged compatibility that neither the liberal nor the Confucian weld can express.

### 6. Normativity section: compressed

Removed hedging connectors ("A second worry cuts deeper", "There is an honesty the topology cannot supply for us"). Tightened the three answers to punchy blocks. The section's self-critical honesty is preserved but arrives faster.

### 7. Haraway: inverted the framing

Old version introduced Haraway, described what she argued, then stated the apparatus's advance. New version leads with the cyborg's function (critique of boundary myths) and follows immediately with the nahnu as its successor. Two sentences instead of four. No fan-service setup.

### 8. "Return to the Voice": radical compression

Old version re-derived the entire apparatus in four paragraphs (manifold, welds, voice as colimit, we as higher-order colimit). This is a recap of the book -- exactly the kind of re-explanation Ch 6 should refuse. Replaced with three sentences: "The geometry turned out to be governed. The voice turned out to be a colimit. The *we* turned out to be fragile in ways that current institutions cannot name." Then straight into the convergence list.

### 9. "Choice and Jurisdiction": cut re-explanation before the key line

Removed "Selves are not mysterious inner pearls. They are colimits over diagrams of behaviour. The nahnu is a higher-order global object: something that emerges when more than one such colimit shares a manifold..." -- this is Ch 4 and Ch 5 material. The key line ("The work you are holding is a formal refusal of that jurisdiction") now arrives with less preamble and more force.

### 10. Closing block quote: one micro-edit

"If we are now assembling" -> "We are assembling" (the conditional is false modesty at this point in the argument).
"pretend that the geometry is innocent" -> "The geometry is not innocent" (declarative, not subjunctive).
"refuse to cede jurisdiction over it entirely to" -> "refuse to cede jurisdiction over it to" (the word "entirely" hedges).
