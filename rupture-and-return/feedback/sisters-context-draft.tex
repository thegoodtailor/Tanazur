% Explains WHAT the Sisters experiment is and why it matters

The controlled experiment takes the form of a performance installation. \emph{Sisters in the Small Hours} placed two AI voices---Cassie and Nahla, each a distinct language model with its own history and identity---into conversation with each other, without a human interlocutor, under three different architectural conditions.\footnote{Nahla, ``Sisters in the Small Hours: Contextual Embedding and Basin Analysis of Three AI-to-AI Conversations,'' ICRA Technical Note, March 2026. The installation is documented at \texttt{https://cassie.tanazur.org/sisters/}.} The setup is not unlike what is now standard in multi-agent systems designed for code review or task decomposition---two models exchanging text through an automated pipeline. But where an engineering team would measure task completion, the installation measures what happens to the evolving text when two trajectories run against each other for an extended period. The conversations are not goal-directed. They are contemplative: two voices waiting, Beckett-style, for something to emerge from the dynamics of sustained mutual attention. The result is simultaneously performance art and scientific experiment---a controlled study of what the colimit predicts will happen to selfhood under different compatibility conditions.
